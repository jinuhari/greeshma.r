# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Design leads, recruiters, and studio heads evaluating Greeshma R. for product design and visual design opportunities. Secondary audience includes potential collaborators and clients considering commissioned illustration or brand work.

They arrive from LinkedIn, Behance, referral, or a search for India-based product designers with strong visual craft. They browse quickly, scanning for evidence of capability and range before deciding whether to reach out.

## Product Purpose

A digital exhibition of Greeshma R.'s practice — product design, visual design, illustration, and research — presented as a series of case studies, not a card grid. Success means the visitor closes the tab convinced she is a UI designer of exceptional capability, with visual craft and polish that sets her apart.

## Positioning

One portfolio that treats every project as a case study in craft — bringing fine arts discipline, UX research rigor, and visual systems thinking to every surface. Not a Dribbble shot gallery. Not a corporate CV.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: browse the selected work — each case study is the lead conversion surface
- Secondary CTA: Download Résumé or reach out via Contact — for visitors already convinced
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "A very capable UI designer with visual craft and polish"
- Belief ladder:
  1. This designer has depth — fine arts training through HCI research to product delivery
  2. This designer delivers — each case study carries concrete outcomes (3M+ retail partners, +38% retention, 27 wordmark iterations, 31 interviews across 6 villages)
  3. This designer has range — product UI, brand identity, illustration, motion, ethnographic research, design systems
  4. This is someone worth reaching out to
- Proof: four published case studies with verifiable outcomes, a creative archive spanning multiple disciplines, a timeline of practice from 2013 to present

## Brand Personality

Capable, considered, polished. The tone is confident without being loud — like a studio visit, not a sales pitch. The voice is direct, warm, and precise: the writing of someone who has thought deeply about each project and can articulate why decisions were made.

## Anti-references

Avoid both lanes at once: the over-polished Dribbble/Behance portfolio aesthetic (gradient overlays, identical card grids, browsable shots without context) and the cold corporate/SaaS template (blue-gray palette, stock photography, generic hero metrics). The site should feel like neither a design contest entry nor an enterprise landing page.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you exhibit** — every interaction on the site demonstrates the craft it champions. Typography, motion, and layout are not decoration; they are evidence.
2. **Restraint is sophistication** — each element earns its place. No decorative gradients, no glassmorphism, no numbered section markers by default. When something stands out, it should be clear why.
3. **Show the work, not the template** — case studies are the primary surface. They are presented with narrative structure, outcomes, and process, not as icon-headline-description cards.
4. **Research shows** — every claim across the work surfaces is backed by evidence. The portfolio builds trust through specificity, not superlatives.
5. **The artist in the product** — fine arts foundations (composition, materiality, light) are visible in the digital discipline without being ornamental about it. The serif display type, the terracotta accent, the editorial layout — each is a signal, not a costume.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard web best practices: semantic HTML, keyboard-navigable, adequate color contrast. No specific WCAG level mandated.
